What Is Buddhism?

Buddhism is a religious and philosophical tradition that originated in ancient India. It is based on the teachings of Siddhartha Gautama, known as the Buddha.

Buddha means "Awakened One." Buddhism teaches wisdom, ethical conduct, mindfulness and compassion.

Who Was Gautama Buddha?

According to Buddhist tradition, Siddhartha Gautama was born at Lumbini, in present-day Nepal.

He sought to understand human suffering. After years of spiritual practice and meditation, he attained enlightenment and taught others the path to liberation.

The Four Noble Truths

1. The Truth of Dukkha

Life as ordinarily experienced involves suffering and dissatisfaction.

2. The Origin of Dukkha

Craving is a central cause of suffering.

3. The Cessation of Dukkha

Suffering can cease when craving ends.

4. The Path to Cessation

The Noble Eightfold Path guides practice toward the cessation of suffering.

The Noble Eightfold Path

Wisdom

Right View and Right Intention.

Ethical Conduct

Right Speech, Right Action and Right Livelihood.

Mental Discipline

Right Effort, Right Mindfulness and Right Concentration.

Important Buddhist Values

  • Compassion toward living beings.
  • Mindfulness and awareness.
  • Wisdom and understanding.
  • Ethical conduct and non-harming.
  • Understanding impermanence.

Major Buddhist Traditions

Theravada

A major tradition practiced in Sri Lanka and several Southeast Asian countries.

Mahayana

A broad tradition practiced in countries including China, Japan, Korea and Vietnam.

Vajrayana

A tradition associated particularly with Tibetan and Himalayan Buddhism.
